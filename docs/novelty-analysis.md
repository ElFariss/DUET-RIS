# Novelty analysis (adversarial review)

Written 2026-11-06 from the repository state and literature searches of the same day. This is an assessment of claims, not a result. It makes no prediction about the matched-config experiment, which has not produced output (`outputs/runs/matched_*` do not exist). Numbers quoted from this repository come only from the local `REPORT.md` files (older than the unverified `handsoff.md` figures from the down node 3060-2).

## Verdict

As a reviewer who knows the field, I would reject every algorithmic novelty claim currently on the table. Neural guidance of an evolutionary or local search for binary RIS control, physics-conditioned per-atom scoring, rank-k Woodbury rescoring of remembered configurations, restricted-neighbourhood 2-opt, and "predict CSI with a recurrent net, then optimise" each have named prior art, several of them in RIS specifically (Stylianopoulos 2022; Stamatelis 2024; Prod'homme and del Hougne 2023; Hammami et al. 2026; Xu 2021; Koralege 2025). The coupled multiport NEC-2 reference, the deadline accounting, and the joint spatial/temporal benchmark are setting or combination differences (WEAK). Nothing is novel as an algorithm today, and the existing development data do not even show a benefit (pair search beats every guided variant in both local reports; the temporal GRU does not beat linear extrapolation). What can still become a defensible, publishable contribution is empirical and methodological: a held-out, budget-matched, complete-runtime comparison of learned versus analytical atom ranking inside the same memory-based search on a coupled 1-bit RIS, reported with equivalence margins. That is a benchmark or negative-result paper, closest to and extending Hammami et al. 2026 (which already concluded that plain coordinate descent is the best trade-off). Whether it is worth a venue slot depends entirely on the matched run, on adding stronger baselines than greedy/pair/random, and on the work being scoped as "when does learning not help" rather than "a new controller".

## What the code does versus what is claimed

These differences matter because reviewers read code and tables, not slogans.

1. The "GA" variants are not genetic algorithms. `_search_matched` in `src/atomwise_ris/optimization/coupled_search.py` is: best-improvement 1-opt climb, rank-2 moves restricted to the top-K atoms by a ranking signal (`moves_subspace`), a random-immigrant escape from all-OFF, rescoring of 4 to 6 remembered patterns via a rank-k Woodbury flip, then 2-bit basin hops taken from the ranking. There is no population, selection or generations. The specular `search.py` has two basins (zeros and ones start) and one two-parent crossover. This is an iterated local search with memory restarts. "Neural guidance of an evolutionary search" is therefore a misdescription and a reviewer will say so.
2. "Mutation" is a neighbourhood restriction. The policy does not propose stochastic edits. It re-ranks atoms (analytical normalised gain plus 0.12 times a normalised neural residual in the coupled case; 0.15 times a logit in the specular case), and an exhaustive rank-2 search runs inside the top K of 64. The neural signal matters only for which marginal atoms enter that subspace.
3. The "no-physics" ablation is not a physics-free control: `attention_no_physics_ga` keeps the exact analytical flip gains (the docs say so). It cannot support a claim that physics conditioning helps.
4. "Memory" is Zardi-style only in spirit. Zardi's ME-RISC stores the best individual in a pool with some probability and restores it into a real GA population; here the last few patterns are rescored on the current channel and climbed. The proposal and matched protocol call it an adaptation, which is accurate.
5. The temporal "velocity residual" is the linear extrapolation `2*x[-1]-x[-2]` plus `0.25*tanh(head)` with a zero-initialised head (`LinkGRU`). The "passivity projection" (`passive_reciprocal`) symmetrises and clips eigenvalues of the real part to a small positive floor. There is no ablation of the projection and no result showing the GRU beats linear extrapolation.
6. Local evidence on rank-ordering: in the coupled report, `attention_ga`, `attention_no_memory_ga`, `mlp_ga`, `memory_ga` and `random` have the identical mean rate (16.765033 bit/s/Hz). That means on those three pilot trajectories the ranking and memory never changed the returned action. This is a degenerate comparison, a warning about the benchmark's power, not a finding about attention.
7. The registered matched contrasts do not include `attention_ga - mlp_ga` or `attention_ga - attention_no_physics_ga`. Any claim that attention specifically, or physics features specifically, matter would be exploratory under the current protocol.

## Notes on the claims with real content

**(i) The only live contribution.** Hammami et al. state that no systematic algorithm comparison existed for discrete, coupling-aware RIS optimisation, then provide one. They report that the cost of a learned or dictionary initialisation is not recovered. So a reviewer will say the headline result (simple search wins) was already published, and the new paper must differ by something real:

- a multiuser worst-user objective instead of SISO gain, a ray-traced scene and a coupled wire reference instead of a statistical generator, and neural ranking policies as well as a surrogate;
- complete CPU runtime, with deadline misses and time-to-target reported;
- trajectories frozen before any test frame existed.

What a strong version needs and the repository lacks: baselines of Hammami's strength (coordinate descent with random restarts, a plain simulated-annealing or tabu search, a true population GA, and one published learned controller such as a RISnet-style network). Today the baselines are greedy, pair and random, and the "GA" variants share the same ILS skeleton by design.

**(j1) Zardi's memory.** Zardi et al. compared only against a GA without memory. The matched design is, in effect, the first test of whether memory is the active ingredient once an exact fast evaluator and an exhaustive pair search exist. The old specular report already has `memory_ga` below `pair` with an interval excluding zero. That is a development-data hint, not evidence.

**(d) as a resource.** The one defensible resource claim is a coupled reference that is open, versioned and mesh-audited. It is only worth stating if the data and generator are actually released, and it needs the coupled-versus-independent caveat the repository already insists on: `specular_v5` and `coupled_wire_v1` differ in geometry and surface physics, so no coupling ablation can be claimed.

## Recommended contribution statement

**Surviving today's evidence (2 to 3 sentences).** We release a simulation protocol for binary RIS controllers that scores spatial updates on held-out trajectories under a hard CPU deadline with complete runtime, and a coupled thin-wire multiport reference with mesh audits. In development data on two simulated scenes, neither an MLP nor an attention ranking of atoms improved on exact analytical pair search at 10 ms, and learned temporal prediction did not beat linear extrapolation, consistent with the conclusion of Hammami et al. for statistical coupled models. We make no claim of a new controller; claims about learned guidance await the held-out matched experiment.

**If the matched experiment is "improved"** (a registered neural contrast, for example `attention_ga - analytical_ga` or `mlp_ga - memory_ga`, has its corrected interval entirely above +0.015 bit/s/Hz): State a conditional, scoped finding: under a shared search skeleton and shared limits, a learned atom ranking raised mean worst-user rate by at least the practical margin over analytical-gain ranking on 30 held-out trajectories of one scene and surface, with deadline misses reported. Do not name it a new algorithm. Before submission also require: `analytical_ga - uniform_ga` (ranking matters at all), the small/large sensitivity configurations leading to the same sign, an `attention` versus `mlp` and `attention` versus no-physics contrast declared as exploratory, a stronger baseline set, and results at more than one array size. A physics-conditioning claim needs an ablation that removes the analytical gains, which does not exist yet.

**If "equivalent"** (the relevant corrected intervals lie entirely within +/-0.015): State a negative result with a quantified margin: replacing analytical-gain ranking with a trained MLP or attention policy changed worst-user rate by less than 0.015 bit/s/Hz on held-out trajectories while consuming inference time from the same deadline, so in these scenes the gains of the memory-based search come from the search structure and exact low-rank evaluation. Say explicitly that the search configuration was fixed from the analytical variant on development data, which favours that variant, and that the margin comes from coupled-wire mesh evidence and is borrowed for the specular scene. This is the strongest honest paper the data can currently support, and its value depends on the baseline set and release of code and data.

**If "inconclusive"** (intervals straddle the margin): No method or equivalence claim. Publish as a protocol and resource report with development-stage observations, or collect more trajectories and seeds under a new pre-registration. In the development data, effect sizes in the specular scene are on the scale of the margin (hundredths of a bit/s/Hz) and the earlier intervals were wide with 6 trajectories, so power, not only the true effect, can produce this outcome. Do not reinterpret an inconclusive result as "no difference".

## Risks a reviewer would raise

1. Not a GA, and "mutation" is neighbourhood pruning. The framing "neural guidance of evolutionary search" will be challenged on the code.
2. Weak baselines. Greedy, exhaustive pair and random are not the strongest published competitors for coupled 1-bit RIS (coordinate descent with restarts, annealing, tabu, a real GA, RISnet-class learned control).
3. Tuning asymmetry. The shared "medium" configuration is the former `analytical_ga` setting, chosen on the development pilot; the neural residual weights (0.12 coupled, 0.15 specular) and the training distribution (random parents and single-flip optima, not the online memory states) were not tuned for the neural variants. Equivalence may be the artefact of that.
4. Degenerate and low-power comparisons. Identical rates across five variants in the coupled report show the benchmark often cannot separate controllers. With a 0.015 margin and 30 trajectories, "inconclusive" is plausible. The decision rule also mixes Holm-corrected p-values with intervals; state how the corrected intervals are formed.
5. Deadline metrics are partly by construction. Anytime controllers that stop at an internal safety margin report p95 near 90% of the budget and zero misses. Python and Torch timing on one pinned core says little about an MCU or FPGA; the paper must not suggest otherwise. Time-to-target uses a reference built from the best result of any method and seed, i.e. test data.
6. Simulation only; independent-cell scalar model with digitised uniform-panel responses for the specular scene; free-space PEC wires without substrate, ground plane, switches or loss for the coupled scene; one scene each; held-out trajectories are not held-out environments; 64 atoms, 2 users.
7. The 1% mesh criterion does not bound rate error (0.72 bit/s/Hz seen at 0.68% channel change on one frame); high-SNR, short-range, high-power geometry makes interference cancellation hypersensitive. Rankings of near-tied controllers could change with mesh or noise.
8. Temporal part: perfect multiport impedance history is not obtainable online; three development trajectories; GRU not better than linear; the stale-CSI oracle (current CSI pair search) dominates by about 10 bit/s/Hz, so forecast quality is not the bottleneck being tested.
9. "Neural inference fraction = 1.0" and the neural budget accounting: inference is excluded from the evaluation count but charged to time. Defensible, but state it next to every table.
10. Reproducibility: the matched run uses a different node and fresh checkpoints; the handoff numbers from the down node must not leak into the paper.

## Things to do before claiming anything
